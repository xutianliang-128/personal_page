I am a Ph.D. candidate in computational social science at the University of Michigan, working with [Prof. Sabina Tomkins](https://www.si.umich.edu/people/sabina-tomkins). My research focuses on understanding how social media platforms shape public discourse, trust in institutions, and information consumption patterns. I employ computational methods and machine learning techniques to analyze large-scale social media data and uncover patterns in how people engage with content online.

Research Interests
======
My research focuses on three interconnected areas:

* **Natural Language Processing (NLP)**: I develop and apply NLP techniques to analyze large-scale text data from social media, government documents, and online platforms. My work includes sentiment analysis, topic modeling, and extracting meaningful insights from unstructured text.

* **Computational Social Science**: I use computational methods to understand social phenomena, including how information spreads, how trust in institutions evolves, and how people engage with content online. I analyze patterns in social media data to uncover mechanisms that shape public discourse and collective behavior.

* **AI Applications**: I build practical AI systems that address real-world challenges, such as helping consumers make informed decisions about product sustainability, making government processes more transparent, and understanding how expertise and influence manifest in online spaces.

Current Work
======
I am currently working on research that examines anti-establishment sentiment across different topical domains on TikTok. Using the TikTok Research API, I've analyzed over 26,000 video posts to understand how distrust in institutions like media and healthcare manifests in different types of content. This work has been featured in [University of Michigan News](https://news.umich.edu/conspiracy-content-drives-anti-establishment-sentiment-on-tiktok-youtube/) and will be presented at ICWSM-26.

I also serve as a Graduate Student Instructor for SI 315: Models of Social Information Processing at the University of Michigan School of Information, where I help students understand how social networks form, interact, and produce collective effects.

You can find more details about my publications, teaching, and other activities in the sections above. Feel free to reach out if you'd like to collaborate or discuss my research!
