A study led by University of Michigan researchers reveals that conspiracy content drives anti-establishment sentiment on TikTok and YouTube. The research, which analyzed 26,783 video posts with 206,350 comments collected over two years using the TikTok Research API, found that people actively seeking conspiracy content involving anti-establishment sentiment—distrust in institutions like the media or health care—will find it regularly on these platforms. However, such sentiment rarely appears in finance, wellness, or the general For You Page, meaning casual users are unlikely to encounter it often. The study found anti-establishment sentiment in about 45% of conspiracy posts, compared to just 4% in finance and 1% in wellness. Interestingly, most comments agreed with the original posts, even when they contained controversial claims. The findings raise questions about how these messages may shape public trust and influence society in the future. The paper, co-authored with Prof. Sabina Tomkins and Prof. Ariel Hasell, will be shared at the International AAAI Conference on Web and Social Media next spring in Los Angeles.
